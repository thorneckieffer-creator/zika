# Voice packs

The voice area: `assets/audio-*`, `voice.html`, `tools/build_voice_pack.py`, `tools/build_voice_pack_breezy.py`, `tools/build_voice_staged.sh`, `tools/build_readings.py`, `tools/content/readings.json`, and the audio functions in `index.html` (`VOICE_PACKS`, `loadPack`, `playBundled`, `playAudio`, `pyKey`, `setVoice`). How playback falls through from pack to system voice is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#audio-playback).

Three slots in `VOICE_PACKS`: `f` (`audio-tw`, 曉臻, the default), `m` (`audio-tw-m`, 雲哲) and `me` (`audio-me`, Thorne's own cloned voice). Settings offers only the packs whose index file exists. A clip key is the word's Han characters; words that share characters but not sound also have `字@pinyin` keys (`pyKey()` in the app, `py_id()` in the builder), and `speakWord` passes the word's pinyin so the right one plays.

Two builders:

- `tools/build_voice_pack.py` — Microsoft's read-aloud voices through edge-tts. Simple, but the service throttles hard (it stopped after 66 clips in November 2026).
- `tools/build_voice_pack_breezy.py` — BreezyVoice (open source, MediaTek Research): clones the voice on a short reference recording. `voice.html` records that reference in the browser. `tools/build_readings.py` writes `tools/content/readings.json` (the publisher's 注音), which the builder uses to pin pronunciations.

BreezyVoice setup on this Mac lives in `~/.cache/zika-voice/bv/` (Miniforge, a Python 3.10 env with pynini from conda-forge, the BreezyVoice checkout, reference recordings in `prompts/`). Lessons learned the hard way:

- **Run it staged, never as one long job.** An uncapped run on the GPU filled the Mac's memory and pushed it deep into swap. `tools/build_voice_staged.sh` renders 120 clips per process at low priority with a pause between stages, caps the GPU allocator, and stops a stage that grows past 7 GB.
- A script that loads the model needs `if __name__ == '__main__':` (g2pw spawns processes on macOS).
- Pin `ruamel.yaml==0.18.6`; install `openai-whisper` unpinned; `setuptools<81`.
- Do not use the repo's text normaliser on macOS — it converts Traditional to Simplified.
- g2pw costs about 8 s per call regardless of size: batch every text into one call.
- The clone copies the reference's speaking pace as well as its sound.
- Claude cannot hear audio. Whisper listen-back catches gross failures on words of two or more syllables but is unreliable on single syllables; Thorne has to approve the sound by ear before a pack ships.

After building or extending a pack, bump `ASSETS` in `sw.js` and run `node tools/check.mjs`, which reports every word without a clip.

## Tone coach (聲調教練, beta; analysis rebuilt in v52)

A drill mode (`tcoach`, functions prefixed `tc`) that records the learner saying a word, shows the pitch curve of each syllable against the native clip's, and grades each tone. It runs entirely in the browser; no audio leaves the device.

- **Pipeline:** recording → 16 kHz → YIN pitch track over 25 ms windows, gated on the loud part of the take (`tcPitch`) → octave slips repaired by continuity, blips out, short gaps bridged (`tcClean`) → voiced stretches made into one per syllable: stray hiss dropped, close gaps merged, long stretches cut at the dip in loudness, quiet edges trimmed (`tcSyllables`) → each syllable reduced to 16 points (`tcShape`) → a small network, 17 inputs → 10 → 4 tones (`tcClassify`, weights in `TC_NET`) → the verdict against the expected tone (`tcJudge`: `ok`, `close`, `no`). `tcTrack` is the slow half (audio → cleaned track); `tcAnalyse` takes a track and is cheap.
- **Measure, do not guess: `tools/tone_lab.mjs`.** It lifts this code out of `index.html` and runs it on recordings with known tones. `prepare` builds the recordings under `~/.cache/zika-voice/tone-lab` (about 600 MB, not in the repo); `check` scores the app as it stands (40 s); `cv` refits with each voice held out, which is the honest number for a new speaker; `fit --write` refits and installs `TC_NET`. Run `check` and `cv` after any change to the functions above, and refit.
- **The ten voices:** two people from [audio-cmn](https://github.com/hugolpz/audio-cmn) (Chen Wang's 1,660 single syllables; Yue Tan's HSK words, the 492 that are also course words), the bundled 曉臻 clips, 雲哲 (485 clips left in the cache by a pack build), and six macOS voices (美佳, 婷婷, and the Taiwan Eddy, Grandpa, Reed, Sandy). About 17,000 graded syllables.
- **Where it stands (v52).** Held out, a voice is read correctly 72–88% of the time, 80% on average (the two people: 88% and 84%). With the grading rule, a correct tone is marked right 78–91% of the time and a wrong one is let through 6–12%. The classifier shipped in v43 was fitted on 曉臻 alone and scored 63% and 65% on the two people (tone 3 read as tone 2: the synthetic voice never rises at the end of a third tone, people do) and 56% on 雲哲 (a falling tone right 46% of the time).
- **What v52 fixed, each found with the bench:** the cleaner used to move any pitch more than 9.5 semitones from the speaker's middle by an octave, which turned a man's real falling tone into a rising one; a 40 ms window lost the track in the middle of a fast fall; the loudness gate followed the loudest click of the take, which threw away about a third of the voiced frames in Thorne's reference recording; the low dying tail of a syllable outweighed the tone itself; a run of syllables with no pause was cut at its midpoint whatever the loudness did.
- **Speaker normalisation:** pitch is judged against the speaker's own mid pitch and spread. For the native voice these are measured once per pack (`tcNativeStats`, cached in localStorage as `zika_tc_native2_<pack>`). For the learner they are learned from every recording (`S.vp`: frame count, sum, sum of squares, in semitones re 100 Hz); the first run asks for one read-aloud sentence. Until then a take is judged against its own middle, which the classifier was fitted to cope with (about three points worse).
- **Expected tones** come from the word's pinyin with third-tone sandhi applied (`tcExpected`); neutral tones are shown but not graded.
- **State:** `S.tone` holds totals per tone (`by`), a confusion count (`conf`, `"2>3"`), results per two-syllable tone pair (`pair`, `"24"`), and the best score per word (`w`). `S.tone` and `S.vp` both sync and both carry `v:2`: `tcState()` starts either one again if it was made by the first analysis, so an older device syncing its copy back cannot revive it.
- **What the learner gets:** per syllable a verdict, the tone it sounded like, and one line on what to change (`TC_TIP`); where third-tone sandhi changes the tone, the chip says so ("tone 2 (written 3)"); a round of ten words in which up to six practise the weakest tone or tone pair so far (`tcFocus`).
- **The tone-pair map** (`tcPairMap`, v53) closes the Drill menu: the sixteen two-syllable pairs as a 4 × 4 grid with how often each came out right. Tapping a cell starts ten words of that pair (`tcStart('24')`), from the chosen scope or, when that holds fewer than five, from every lesson up to the learner's own. 3 + 3 has no cell: sandhi makes it 2 + 3.
- **Limits, said plainly.** It has still not been validated against a learner: every voice in the bench is a native speaker or a synthetic one, and none is Thorne. Claude cannot hear; the checks are the bench, synthetic glides played into the real recorder in the browser, and the pitch statistics of Thorne's one reference recording. The curves are the real feedback and the grades a guide. 雲哲's and 美佳's second tones stay the weakest cells.
- **Not done:** sentence-level practice; neutral-tone grading; more human voices in the bench, men above all. Two sources are ready to add: Mozilla Common Voice zh-TW, and Lingua Libre on Wikimedia Commons (category *Lingua Libre pronunciation-cmn*: about 880 course words across six more speakers, as WAV). Commons answers an unidentified script with HTTP 429 after a handful of files — it wants a User-Agent with contact details and a slow fetch, which is Thorne's call to give.
