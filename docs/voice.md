# Voice packs

The voice area: `assets/audio-*`, `voice.html`, `tools/build_voice_pack.py`, `tools/build_voice_pack_breezy.py`, `tools/build_voice_staged.sh`, `tools/build_readings.py`, `tools/content/readings.json`, `tools/voice_audit.mjs` and `tools/voice_listen.html` (the packs measured against the answer key, and the page a native listener marks them on), and the audio functions in `js/audio.js` (`VOICE_PACKS`, `loadPack`, `playBundled`, `playAudio`, `pyKey`, `setVoice`). How playback falls through from pack to system voice is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#audio-playback).

Three slots in `VOICE_PACKS`: `f` (`audio-tw`, 曉臻, the default), `m` (`audio-tw-m`, 雲哲) and `me` (`audio-me`, Thorne's own cloned voice). Settings offers only the packs whose index file exists. A clip key is the word's Han characters; words that share characters but not sound also have `字@pinyin` keys (`pyKey()` in the app, `py_id()` in the builder), and `speakWord` passes the word's pinyin so the right one plays.

Two builders:

- `tools/build_voice_pack.py` — Microsoft's read-aloud voices through edge-tts. Simple, but the service throttles hard (it stopped after 66 clips in November 2026).
- `tools/build_voice_pack_breezy.py` — BreezyVoice (open source, MediaTek Research): clones the voice on a short reference recording. `voice.html` records that reference in the browser. `tools/build_readings.py` writes `tools/content/readings.json` (the publisher's 注音), which the builder uses to pin pronunciations.

BreezyVoice setup on this Mac lives in `~/.cache/zika-voice/bv/` (Miniforge, a Python 3.10 env with pynini from conda-forge, the BreezyVoice checkout, reference recordings in `prompts/`). Lessons learned the hard way:

- **Run it staged, never as one long job.** An uncapped run on the GPU filled the Mac's memory and pushed it deep into swap. `tools/build_voice_staged.sh` renders 120 clips per process at low priority with a pause between stages, caps the GPU allocator, and stops a stage that grows past 7 GB.
- A script that loads the model needs `if __name__ == '__main__':` (g2pw spawns processes on macOS).
- Pin `ruamel.yaml==0.18.6`; install `openai-whisper` unpinned; `setuptools<81`.
- Do not use the repo's text normaliser on macOS — it converts Traditional to Simplified.
- g2pw costs about 8 s per call regardless of size: batch every text into one call.
- The clone copies the reference's speaking pace as well as its sound.
- Claude cannot hear audio. Whisper listen-back catches gross failures on words of two or more syllables but is unreliable on single syllables; Thorne has to approve the sound by ear before a pack ships.

After changing a pack that devices may already hold, bump `ASSETS` in `sw.js` (assets are cache-first, so an unchanged name keeps serving the old file). A brand-new pack needs no bump. Either way run `node tools/check.mjs`, which reports every word without a clip.

The 雲哲 pack (`audio-tw-m`, 2,044 clips, every word and character through Book 3 Lesson 4) was built in November 2026 with `tools/build_voice_pack.py --voice zh-TW-YunJheNeural --out audio-tw-m --pause 2.0`. The read-aloud service lets a few dozen requests through and then refuses for a minute or two, so expect about 190 clips an hour; the builder backs off and resumes by itself, and a second run fills any clips that failed.

## The plan: one answer key for the clips and the tone coach

Thorne, 21 November 2026 ([master-plan.md](master-plan.md#voice--one-answer-key-for-the-clips-and-the-tone-coach)). He hears the wrong tone about a third of the time (a third for a first, a second for a fourth). It is not yet known whether the clip plays it wrong, the tone coach grades him wrong, or both.

**Why they go together.** The tone coach grades by comparing the learner's pitch with the native clip. A clip with the wrong tone teaches the wrong sound and marks a correct tone wrong. The goal is one verified native recording for every word, character and phrase, used for listening and as the coach's reference.

**How it fits the rest.** Voice packs are static files, not database records: none of this touches the sync engine, the adapter or accounts, so it runs beside the backend. The "sounds wrong" log is kept on the device, outside the deck. A licensed rebuild and a speaker's written agreement close the voice-rights question that stands before other learners ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md#app), step 12). The personal cloned voice stays on the device only. API keys (Azure, ElevenLabs) live in `~/.config/zika/`, never in the repository.

### V0 — what the audit found

Run on 21 November 2026, tools only (`tools/voice_audit.mjs`, `tools/voice_listen.html`). Nothing in the app changed, and the "sounds wrong" button is not built yet (it is an app change and waits for v66 or v67). **No clip is called wrong here.** Claude cannot hear: the audit says where the analysis and the answer key disagree, and the listening set below is what turns that into an error rate.

**The method, in three lines.**

1. Every clip of both packs is decoded and put through the coach's own code (`tcTrack`, `tcAnalyse`, `tcJudge`, imported from `tools/tone_lab.mjs`, which loads `js/tone-analysis.js` as it is), with each voice's mid pitch and spread measured the way the coach measures its native voice.
2. The tone heard on each syllable is compared with the answer key: the publisher's 注音 (`tools/content/readings.json`) and, as a second witness, the course's pinyin; then sandhi (不 before a fourth tone → second; 一 → second before a fourth, fourth before a first, second or third, not after 第 and not word-final; third before third → second, left to right as `tcExpected` does). The 注音 and the pinyin already write 一 and 不 as spoken, so the first two rules changed no reading. **Neutral tones are not graded** (the classifier has four classes and no neutral one; 83 and 89 syllables), and the 兒 of 一會兒 and 餡兒 counts as neutral.
3. Each syllable is read twice: by the app's classifier, which was fitted on these very clips, and by one fitted without either pack voice. The same two are run on the bench's two people, whose tones are known; that gives the analysis's own error to hold the packs against.

**What it says.**

- **A flag is a suspect, not a verdict.** The analysis names the right tone on 84–88% of a new person's syllables, so a pack with no wrong clip at all would still show 12–15% of its syllables disagreeing. By volume, all of 曉臻's disagreement (13.5%) and about half of 雲哲's (26.3%) could be the analysis's own error.
- **By kind it is not the analysis's usual error.** The confusions it makes on people (4 → 3, 3 → 2, 4 → 1) are rarer here than on people; three or four other pairs are five to fifteen times over. Something systematic sits in those pairs, and measurement cannot say which of two things it is: (a) the voices say these tones in a way a Taiwanese ear accepts and the classifier reads as the neighbouring tone (a second tone that dips before it rises, a third tone that only falls, a first tone that sags at the end of a clip of one character: the bench had already shown that the synthetic voice never rises at the end of a third tone); or (b) those clips sound wrong. The listening set is spread over the pairs to decide this.
- **The two pairs Thorne named are not where the measurement points.** "A third for a first" and "a second for a fourth", either way round, are 29 of 曉臻's 359 disagreements and 108 of 雲哲's 852, at or near the rate the analysis produces on correct speech. What the measurement sees most is a second tone taken for a third (both voices), a third for a fourth (曉臻) and a first for a fourth (雲哲, single characters).
- **The coach's grade does not use the clip.** In the code the verdict is the classifier against the expected tone (`tcJudge` on `tcExpected`, `js/tone.js`); the native clip is only drawn as the reference curve. So a wrong clip cannot mark a right tone wrong. It can teach the wrong sound and draw a misleading curve.
- **The coach's own error is already about the size of what Thorne reports.** The bench's figure for a new voice (a right tone marked right 78–91% of the time) means a two-syllable word said perfectly gets a mark against it somewhere between one time in six and two in five (1 − 0.91² to 1 − 0.78², taking the syllables as independent). Measured here directly: fed the packs' own clips as if they were the learner, the coach fails to pass 14% of 曉臻's clips and 33% of 雲哲's, voices it was fitted on. Whatever the ears find about the clips, the coach's grading is a cause on its own; that is V4's subject.
- **The clearest suspects among the clips are the Taiwan readings.** On 113 syllables (112 words) both voices are measured as the same other tone. Among them: 和 hàn measured second tone in both (what hé would give), 炸 zhá and 喂 wéi measured fourth, 法國 Fǎguó measured fourth-first and fourth-third, 亞 and 亞洲 yà measured third, 下載 xiàzài measured fourth-third. These are in V1's word list, where an ear compares the sources.
- **The answer key has 13 words of its own to settle** before V2 pins any voice to it: the 注音 in `readings.json` and the course's pinyin disagree on 背包 (ㄅㄟˋ / bēi), 暑假 (ㄐㄧㄚˇ / jià), 天分 (ㄈㄣ / fèn), 只要 (ㄓ / zhǐ), 古蹟 (ㄐㄧˋ / jī), 待 (ㄉㄞˋ / dāi), 除了 and 為了 (ㄌㄧㄠˇ / le), 瓶子 (ㄗˇ / zi), 剛剛 (ㄍㄤ / gang), 餓死了 (ㄌㄜ with no dot / le), 一會兒 (ㄏㄨㄟˋ / huǐ) and 餡兒 (兒). The audit accepts either for these. For the other 1,446 words the key and the app's `tcExpected` give the same tones.

**Limits.** The app's classifier was fitted on these clips with these labels (1,164 of 曉臻's, 484 of 雲哲's), so its figures flatter the packs; the held-out one has never heard a voice of this kind and reads 58–61% of their second tones as something else, nearly always a third, which is at least partly the classifier meeting the voice. The yardstick is two people only (one reading single syllables, one a mainland speaker reading words), not a Taiwanese speaker and not a synthetic voice. A cut between syllables in the wrong place shows as a wrong tone, never as a failure: middle and last syllables disagree most. Clips whose key holds several readings (19 and 23) are graded against the one they match best. One clip per pack has no answer in the key (範, KTV).

**The listening set and how to run it.** 60 clips, 30 per voice: 20 flagged (two of each pair Thorne named where the pack has them, the rest in proportion to how often each pair occurs) and 10 unflagged (by length), drawn with a fixed seed from clips with one acceptable reading and one flagged syllable. The page shows the word, 注音, pinyin and the tones as they should be spoken; it does not show which clips are flagged. Two listeners mark each clip right, wrong or not sure, tap the syllable that is wrong and may leave a note; marks stay in that browser and download as a file.

```
node tools/voice_audit.mjs serve                 # then open, in one browser for both listeners:
http://127.0.0.1:8791/tools/voice_listen.html?set=v0
node tools/voice_audit.mjs score ~/Downloads/voice-marks-v0.json
```

`score` prints, per listener, how often the analysis and the ear agree, how often a flag is a wrong clip, how often an unflagged clip is wrong, and from those a rough error rate for each pack: the number the V0 row asks for. `node tools/voice_audit.mjs run` measures the packs again (about a minute; `clips.csv`, `clips.json`, `summary.md` and `summary.json` in `~/.cache/zika-voice/audit/`, one row per clip with expected and measured tones, the confidence of each syllable and the flags); `listen` draws the set again; `pagetest` opens the page in headless Chrome and checks that it loads, plays, marks, downloads and hides the flags.

## Tone coach (聲調教練, beta; analysis rebuilt in v52)

A drill mode (`tcoach`, functions prefixed `tc`) that records the learner saying a word, shows the pitch curve of each syllable against the native clip's, and grades each tone. It runs entirely in the browser; no audio leaves the device.

- **Pipeline:** recording → 16 kHz → YIN pitch track over 25 ms windows, gated on the loud part of the take (`tcPitch`) → octave slips repaired by continuity, blips out, short gaps bridged (`tcClean`) → voiced stretches made into one per syllable: stray hiss dropped, close gaps merged, long stretches cut at the dip in loudness, quiet edges trimmed (`tcSyllables`) → each syllable reduced to 16 points (`tcShape`) → a small network, 17 inputs → 10 → 4 tones (`tcClassify`, weights in `TC_NET`) → the verdict against the expected tone (`tcJudge`: `ok`, `close`, `no`). `tcTrack` is the slow half (audio → cleaned track); `tcAnalyse` takes a track and is cheap.
- **Measure, do not guess: `tools/tone_lab.mjs`.** It loads `js/text.js` and `js/tone-analysis.js` as they are and runs that code on recordings with known tones. `prepare` builds the recordings under `~/.cache/zika-voice/tone-lab` (about 600 MB, not in the repo); `check` scores the app as it stands (40 s); `cv` refits with each voice held out, which is the honest number for a new speaker; `fit --write` refits and installs `TC_NET`. Run `check` and `cv` after any change to the functions above, and refit.
- **Every release is guarded too.** `node tools/check.mjs` synthesises the four tones at a low, a middle and a high voice, alone and in pairs (with a pause and run together), puts them through the same code, and fails the release if any is misheard; it also feeds the cleaner a fall of more than an octave (must be kept) and a creaky octave flicker (must be repaired). It needs no recordings and takes a second. Breaking the tracker's low range, or putting back the first cleaner's rule, both fail it. One thing it showed: a third tone that climbs all the way back to where it started is let through as a second tone by the grading rule.
- **The ten voices:** two people from [audio-cmn](https://github.com/hugolpz/audio-cmn) (Chen Wang's 1,660 single syllables; Yue Tan's HSK words, the 492 that are also course words), the bundled 曉臻 clips, 雲哲 (485 clips left in the cache by a pack build), and six macOS voices (美佳, 婷婷, and the Taiwan Eddy, Grandpa, Reed, Sandy). About 17,000 graded syllables.
- **Where it stands (v52).** Held out, a voice is read correctly 72–88% of the time, 80% on average (the two people: 88% and 84%). With the grading rule, a correct tone is marked right 78–91% of the time and a wrong one is let through 6–12%. The classifier shipped in v43 was fitted on 曉臻 alone and scored 63% and 65% on the two people (tone 3 read as tone 2: the synthetic voice never rises at the end of a third tone, people do) and 56% on 雲哲 (a falling tone right 46% of the time).
- **What v52 fixed, each found with the bench:** the cleaner used to move any pitch more than 9.5 semitones from the speaker's middle by an octave, which turned a man's real falling tone into a rising one; a 40 ms window lost the track in the middle of a fast fall; the loudness gate followed the loudest click of the take, which threw away about a third of the voiced frames in Thorne's reference recording; the low dying tail of a syllable outweighed the tone itself; a run of syllables with no pause was cut at its midpoint whatever the loudness did.
- **Speaker normalisation:** pitch is judged against the speaker's own mid pitch and spread. For the native voice these are measured once per pack (`tcNativeStats`, cached in localStorage as `zika_tc_native2_<pack>`). For the learner they are learned from every recording (`S.vp`: frame count, sum, sum of squares, in semitones re 100 Hz); the first run asks for one read-aloud sentence. Until then a take is judged against its own middle, which the classifier was fitted to cope with (about three points worse).
- **Expected tones** come from the word's pinyin with third-tone sandhi applied (`tcExpected`); neutral tones are shown but not graded.
- **State:** `S.tone` holds totals per tone (`by`), a confusion count (`conf`, `"2>3"`), results per two-syllable tone pair (`pair`, `"24"`), and the best score per word (`w`). `S.tone` and `S.vp` both sync and both carry `v:2`: `tcState()` starts either one again if it was made by the first analysis, so an older device syncing its copy back cannot revive it.
- **What the learner gets:** per syllable a verdict, the tone it sounded like, and one line on what to change (`TC_TIP`); where third-tone sandhi changes the tone, the chip says so ("tone 2 (written 3)"); a round of ten words in which up to six practise the weakest tone or tone pair so far (`tcFocus`).
- **From a review card** (v54): the answer side of a word card has a microphone beside the speaker (`tcMicBtn`). It opens the same coach for that one word in a sheet (`tcQuick`; `TC.host` names the element `tcRender` paints into, and is null in the Drill tab). Closing the sheet, by its button or a tap outside it, releases the microphone; while it is open the review's keys do not grade the card behind it.
- **Short phrases** (v56): `tcStart('phr')` runs ten phrases instead of ten words — the usage lines (`USAGE`) of the words in scope that are two to four characters, nothing but characters, and more than the headword (`tcPhrases`; about seventy for a lesson). A phrase is an item made for the round (`TC.items`, id `p:w12:0`, read through `tcItem`), not a word: it has no clip, so there is no native curve and the system voice reads it, and it is not recorded in `S.tone.w`. Its tones still count in the totals and the pair map. The buttons are on the "Your tones" panel and at the end of a round.
- **Keys** (v57, for the Mac): Space records and stops, Enter moves on, A tries again, L listens, Esc closes the sheet. One capture-phase listener beside `tcQuickClose`; it acts only while the coach is on screen and swallows only the keys it uses.
- **The tone-pair map** (`tcPairMap`, v53) closes the Drill menu: the sixteen two-syllable pairs as a 4 × 4 grid with how often each came out right. Tapping a cell starts ten words of that pair (`tcStart('24')`), from the chosen scope or, when that holds fewer than five, from every lesson up to the learner's own. 3 + 3 has no cell: sandhi makes it 2 + 3.
- **Tried, and not worth repeating:** telling the classifier where a syllable sits in its word (first, last) changed nothing; a wider or narrower search window for the cut between syllables, a different pull towards the midpoint, and other edge-trim levels all land within a point of the shipped settings (cutting at the loudness dip is worth about one point over cutting at the midpoint); a larger or smaller network (6 to 24 hidden units) scores the same held out. Placing a new learner's mid pitch from the native clip of the same word, before the app has a voice profile, marks more right tones right (+4 points) and lets as many more wrong ones through, so it was left out. What would move the number is more voices, not more tuning.
- **The expected tones were checked too:** the course's pinyin already writes 一 and 不 with the tone they are spoken with (48 cases, none wrong; 第一手 keeps yī, as it should), so `tcExpected` only has to add third-tone sandhi.
- **Rooms are not studios.** The human word recordings were run again with half a second of room either side and noise mixed in: white noise at 20 dB and at 10 dB below the voice costs one or two points (85% → 84%, 83%), a finger's thump on Record costs nothing, mains hum 20 dB below the voice costs one point, and hum only 10 dB below it costs thirteen (the tracker follows the hum between syllables). A steady loud drone is the thing to keep away from the microphone.
- **Quiet microphones.** The coach records with the device's automatic gain off (it would bend the loudness the syllable cutter relies on), so levels can be low. The human recordings turned down to a tenth and to a thirtieth of their level score the same as at full level; at a hundredth they lose twelve points and a fifth of the words cannot be cut into syllables. So a take whose loud part is under `TC_QUIET` (`tcLoud`, v58) is not graded: the coach asks for it again, closer or louder. The same applies to the calibration sentence.
- **Limits, said plainly.** It has still not been validated against a learner: every voice in the bench is a native speaker or a synthetic one, and none is Thorne. Claude cannot hear; the checks are the bench, synthetic glides played into the real recorder in the browser, and the pitch statistics of Thorne's one reference recording. The curves are the real feedback and the grades a guide. 雲哲's and 美佳's second tones stay the weakest cells.
- **What 97% would take (measured 2026-11-15, after Thorne asked for it).** A scratch copy of the bench was given richer inputs and re-run with each voice held out:
  - *The ceiling of this design is about 91%.* A 64-unit network fitted **and scored on the same data** reads 91.5% of tones right. The pitch contour that reaches the classifier does not hold more than that, whatever is fitted to it, so 97% needs a different front end, not a better fit.
  - *Context and loudness are worth three to four points.* Adding five points of each neighbouring syllable's contour, the gaps either side, the word's length, eight points of the syllable's loudness and its untrimmed first and last pitch takes the held-out mean from 81.1% to 84.2% (the two people 86.7% → 89.9%; tone 2 73% → 83%, tone 3 73% → 81%). Telling it the tones the neighbours should have adds nothing more (84.5%). Not installed yet: it changes `tcClassify`'s inputs, the bench and the release self-test together.
  - *Last syllables are the weak place:* 75% right against 85% for the first syllable of a word (a second tone at the end of a word: 65%).
  - *Some labels are the bench's fault.* 41 word-syllables are read as the same other tone by most voices, nearly all Taiwan readings that the mainland voices say their own way (炸 zhá, 喂 wéi, 和 hàn, 星期 xīngqí, 垃圾 lèsè, 企業 qìyè, 研究 yánjiù, 法國 Fǎguó) or a neutral tone the course writes in full (舒服, 不好意思). They hold 7% of the word errors, about one point: worth excluding per voice, not the main story.
  - *Sure and unsure.* If the coach only commits when its top probability is high, the verdicts it gives are 97% right on the surest 85% of a held-out person's single syllables, on the surest 61% of the two people's syllables overall, and on the surest 34% across every voice (richer inputs; 59% and 7% for the last two as shipped).
  - *What others reach.* A network that listens to the sound itself (a mel spectrogram of the syllable) instead of a pitch contour is reported at 99.2% on isolated syllables ([ToneNet, Interspeech 2019](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2019-1483)); in connected speech the reported figures are 88% ([Lugosch & Tomar 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02465), AISHELL-1) and 92.6% ([Tang & Li 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05657), AISHELL-3). So 97% is a known result for a syllable said alone and above anything published for syllables inside running speech. The training data exists and is free: [AISHELL-3](https://openslr.org/93/) (85 hours, 218 speakers, pinyin with tones, Apache 2.0, a 19 GB download).
  - *The route, in order:* (1) the richer inputs and a "not sure" verdict in the present design; (2) the bench's labels cleaned and its last-syllable losses traced; (3) a small spectrogram network trained on AISHELL-3 and run in the browser, with the pitch curves kept as the picture the learner sees. Step 3 is a download and a long training run on Thorne's Mac: ask first.
- **Not done:** whole sentences (phrases stop at four syllables because cutting a longer run into syllables is not reliable enough: three- and four-syllable words score 79% and 78% in the bench against 83% for two); neutral-tone grading; more human voices in the bench, men above all.
